How it works

  1. Flights: we look at which airline flights actually flew your route on the same weekday last week (OpenSky Network) and assume they operate again, usually with the same aircraft type.
  2. Turbulence: each flight's path (great circle, typical cruise altitude) is checked against the latest NOAA turbulence forecast at the time the plane will be there.
  3. Aircraft: bigger, heavier planes feel the same air as less bumpy, so we adjust for aircraft type.
  4. Seats: seats over the wing, near the plane's centre of gravity, move the least.

For comfort planning only, not a safety tool. Forecasts cover roughly the next 36 hours, and pilots routinely change altitude or route to avoid turbulence. Aircraft types and times are based on last week's operations and can change. Climb and descent are not scored.

Data: NOAA/NWS Aviation Weather Center, NOAA NCEP, OpenSky Network, OurAirports, OpenFlights. Map © OpenStreetMap contributors © CARTO.
